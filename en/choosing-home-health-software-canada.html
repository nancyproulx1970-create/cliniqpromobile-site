How to choose software for your home health practice in Canada (2026)

Short answer: for a self-employed health practice anywhere in Canada, check seven things before choosing: data hosted in Canada, signed notes that can no longer be changed, receipts with your licence number, travel and mileage, expenses and your accountant, the real total price, and language. Prices below were checked on the vendors' websites on November 15, 2026.

The 7 things to check

  • Data in Canada. Privacy laws apply in every province (Law 25 in Quebec). Ask where the data is hosted.
  • Signed, locked notes. A signed note should no longer be editable; corrections are added beside it, dated. An access log should record every time a record is opened.
  • Receipts ready for insurance. Your regulatory body and licence number on every receipt, with the right taxes for your province.
  • Travel. If you work in patients' homes, scheduling by location and automatic mileage save you a separate logbook.
  • Expenses and your accountant. Receipt photos and a year-end summary, or you need a second tool.
  • The real total price. Price per practitioner, minimum users, appointment limits, paid add-ons: add it all up.
  • Language. English, French or both, in the app and in support.
