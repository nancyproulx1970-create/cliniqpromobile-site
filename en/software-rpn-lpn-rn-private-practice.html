Software for RPNs, LPNs and RNs in private practice: what your college expects, province by province

Short answer: in private practice, you are the one who keeps the records, sets the fees and gives the receipts. Your college sets the rules: how long to keep a record, what a receipt lists, what clients must be told in advance. Here is what we read on each college's site for Ontario, Alberta, British Columbia and Nova Scotia, and where each point lives in CliniQ Pro, software and app for self-employed health professionals.

RPN, LPN, RN: same work, different names

  • RPN (Registered Practical Nurse) is the Ontario title. Elsewhere in Canada the title is LPN (Licensed Practical Nurse). In Quebec it is infirmière auxiliaire or infirmier auxiliaire.
  • In Western Canada, the Yukon and, since December 1, 2025, Nova Scotia, RPN also stands for Registered Psychiatric Nurse, a separate profession.
  • RN (Registered Nurse) is used across Canada.
  • In Ontario, a chiropodist is a regulated foot specialist. A mobile foot care nurse is an RN or RPN who gives nursing foot care, not a chiropodist.
  • RMT is a Registered Massage Therapist.

Registered massage therapists (Ontario)

The College of Massage Therapists of Ontario (CMTO) standards we read ask RMTs to communicate fees before providing services, display fees in the practice setting, inform patients of any penalty for missed or cancelled appointments before the first appointment, and issue receipts with the date, the patient's name, the amount, the length of the appointment, the HST number if applicable, and the RMT's name, signature and registration number. Records are kept 10 years after the patient's last visit (for a minor, 10 years after the 18th birthday), with a daily appointment record.

What stays in your hands

Software does not make a practice compliant on its own. Telling clients your fees and your missed-appointment policy, writing your record policies, carrying liability protection and following your college's advertising rules remain your responsibility.

Frequently asked questions

How long must a self-employed nurse keep client records?

It depends on the province. Ontario's CNO guideline says at least 10 years after the nurse-client relationship ends. Alberta's LPN guideline says at least 10 years after the last entry. British Columbia's BCCNM bylaws say at least 16 years from the last entry or from the date the client turns 19.

Is an RPN in Ontario the same as an LPN?

Yes. Ontario calls its practical nurses Registered Practical Nurses (RPN). The other provinces use Licensed Practical Nurse (LPN). In Western Canada, the Yukon and Nova Scotia, RPN also means Registered Psychiatric Nurse, a different profession.

Do I have to tell clients my fees in advance?

In Ontario, the CNO expects nurses in independent practice to inform clients in advance of their fees, payment methods and any fee for missed appointments. Alberta's CRNA says the same about fees and payment methods for RNs. Check your own college.
