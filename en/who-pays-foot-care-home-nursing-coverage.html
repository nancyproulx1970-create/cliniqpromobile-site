Who pays for nursing foot care and home nursing? VAC, PSHCP and Blue Cross (2026)

Short answer: three payers come up often for foot care and home nursing. Veterans Affairs Canada (VAC) pays for nursing foot care, with pre-approval. The Public Service Health Care Plan (PSHCP) reimburses 80% of prescribed nursing services, up to $20,000 per calendar year. Blue Cross pays according to each person's contract.

Veterans Affairs Canada (VAC)

VAC nursing services are Program of Choice (POC) 8. They include nursing assessments, nursing visits and nursing foot care. The policy in effect since January 21, 2026 says:

  • nursing foot care is performed by a nurse working within their scope of practice, or who has additional foot care training and the matching credential;
  • nursing services require pre-approval, as stated in the benefit grids;
  • a prescription is required for advanced foot care only, which is approved in exceptional circumstances.

VAC lists Licensed/Registered Practical Nurses and Registered Nurses among the health professionals it covers. Medavie Blue Cross registers providers for VAC. Once registered, you verify the client's eligibility, get authorization before the service and are paid directly. If the Veteran pays you directly, they send their original paid receipts to VAC's National Reimbursement Centre.

A Medavie Blue Cross provider bulletin (August 2023) states that foot care in a clinic is the first option, and that foot care in the client's home is provided when necessary. The number of visits and the fee paid vary by province: they are in the benefit grids, which Medavie Blue Cross gives to providers.

Public Service Health Care Plan (PSHCP)

The PSHCP covers federal public service employees and retirees. Canada Life administers it.

  • Nursing services: reimbursed at 80%, up to $20,000 in eligible expenses per calendar year (since January 1, 2024). The care must be prescribed by a physician, or by a nurse practitioner where the law allows it, and given in the member's home. Registered nurses, registered practical nurses and licensed practical nurses qualify. The plan also requires that the care be medically necessary, that it require the skills of a private-duty nurse, and that the physician first complete a Canada Life questionnaire. Each claim is assessed case by case.
  • Podiatrist or chiropodist: 80%, up to $500 per calendar year, combined. Foot care given by a licensed nurse in a community nursing station is reimbursed under this benefit.
  • Foot care at home: the plan states that foot care costs may also be eligible if the treatment has been prescribed by a physician and given in the person's home by a private-duty nurse, under the nursing services conditions.

The page we read does not say under which maximum foot care at home is processed. The client should ask Canada Life before the visit.

Blue Cross

Blue Cross sells group and individual plans. Each contract has its own list of covered care. Here is one example, to show how to read a contract: the individual "Complete Health" plan offered to Quebec residents (brochure dated September 2025).

BenefitEntryEssentialEnhanced
Nursing careNot covered$3,500 per 2 calendar years$5,600 per 2 calendar years
Chiropodist or podiatrist$40 per visit, up to $250 per calendar year$55 per visit, up to $400$70 per visit, up to $500

The policy for this plan adds that nursing care needs a prescription, must be given at the insured person's home and must be pre-approved by Blue Cross. It names the registered nurse, the registered nursing assistant and the licensed practical nurse. It does not name foot care given by a nurse: that is a question for the insurer.

Medavie Blue Cross's nursing care claim form asks for a statement from the physician who prescribed the care, then the provider's name, designation, registration number, date, hours, amount and signature. Original receipts, paid in full, are attached.

Three questions to ask before the first visit

The insured person is the one who calls the insurer. You can hand them these three questions:

  • Does my contract cover this care, given by a member of this profession?
  • Do I need a prescription or an approval before the care?
  • What is the maximum, per visit and per year?

Frequently asked questions

Does Veterans Affairs Canada pay for foot care given by a nurse?

Yes. Nursing foot care is part of VAC nursing services (Program of Choice 8). It needs pre-approval. A prescription is required for advanced foot care only.

Does the PSHCP cover home nursing?

Yes, under conditions: at 80%, up to $20,000 in eligible expenses per calendar year, when the care is prescribed by a physician (or a nurse practitioner where the law allows it) and given in the member's home.

Does Blue Cross cover foot care given by an LPN or RPN?

It depends on the contract. The plans we read name nursing care (with a prescription) and the chiropodist or podiatrist, but not foot care given by a nurse. The client should ask the insurer before the visit.

What must the receipt show?

The name of the person who received care, the date, the service, the amount paid, your name, your title, your college and your registration number. Our insurance receipt guide has the details.
