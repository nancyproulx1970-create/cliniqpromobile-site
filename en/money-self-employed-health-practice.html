The money side of self-employed practice: cancellations, travel fees, deposits, CPP and QPP (2026)

Short answer: when you work for yourself, you tell clients your fees in advance (care, travel, missed appointments), you give a receipt for every payment and you pay both shares of the pension contribution: 11.9% to the CPP, or 12.6% to the QPP in Quebec, in 2026. Incorporating is not required.

Your cancellation policy

Write it in two or three sentences: how much notice cancels at no charge, the amount charged after that, and the exceptions (illness, hospital stay). Share it before the first appointment.

Colleges ask for this. In Ontario, the College of Nurses (CNO) expects clients to be informed in advance of fees, payment methods and fees for missed appointments. The College of Massage Therapists of Ontario (CMTO) asks RMTs to inform patients of any penalty before the first appointment. Check your own college's rule.

A missed appointment is not care. The document you give must describe what really happened.

Travel fees

If you charge a travel fee, announce it in advance like your other fees, and show it on its own line. Ask your accountant whether that fee is taxable in your case. On your side, driving for work is a business expense: see the mileage deduction.

Deposits from clients

A deposit is an amount paid before the care. Colleges set limits. In Ontario, for example, the CNO says not to charge in advance for services not yet provided, unless they are part of a treatment plan, with reasonable refund policies. Check with your college before asking for one.

Income tax instalments

These are something else: income tax payments made during the year. According to the CRA, you may have to pay instalments for 2026 if your net tax owing is more than $3,000 ($1,800 for Quebec residents) in 2026 and in 2025 or 2024. If you live in Quebec, you also pay instalments to Revenu Québec, under its own rules. In your first year, set money aside for tax and contributions.

CPP and QPP: you pay both shares

An employee splits the contribution with the employer. When you are self-employed, you pay both shares. The contribution is calculated on your income tax return.

Incorporating or not

  • Sole proprietorship: the business has no legal existence separate from you. You keep the profits and bear the losses.
  • Corporation: a separate legal entity that owns property in its own name. Shareholders are not responsible for its debts, and it is taxed separately from its owners.

A corporation does not change your professional responsibility. In Ontario, the CNO guideline describes the health professional corporation as an option, not a requirement, and suggests consulting an accountant or a lawyer. In Quebec, the OIIAQ states that practical nurses remain fully responsible for their professional acts, may incorporate without its authorization, and must carry professional liability insurance for the corporation.

Interac e-Transfer

Interac e-Transfer is handy for home visits: no terminal, no cash. According to Interac, fees and limits are set by your financial institution, and Autodeposit lets funds arrive without a security question. Three simple habits: receive transfers in an account separate from your personal finances, never write health information in the transfer message, and give a receipt as you would for any other payment.

Starting out after school

  • Your registration first: you must be a member in good standing of your college. Many colleges require you to notify them of any new practice location, including self-employed and home practice: check with yours.
  • Your liability protection: ask your college and your insurer what is covered when you work for yourself.
  • A separate account: the CRA recommends a separate account to keep personal and business finances apart.
  • Your records: the CRA asks you to keep records of all your transactions, generally for six years from the end of the tax year they relate to.
  • Your fees and your cancellation policy, written before the first appointment.

Frequently asked questions

How much CPP does a self-employed person pay in 2026?

11.9% of earnings between $3,500 and $74,600, up to $8,460.90, then 8% between $74,600 and $85,000, up to $832, according to Canada.ca.

And QPP, in Quebec?

12.6% of employment earnings between $3,500 and $74,600, then 8% between $74,600 and $85,000. The maximum for the year is $9,791, according to Retraite Québec.

Can I charge for a missed appointment?

It depends on your college. In Ontario, for example, the College of Nurses expects clients to be told in advance about any fee for missed appointments. Write your policy down and share it before the first appointment.

Do I have to incorporate to work for myself?

No, it is not required. A sole proprietorship is the simplest form. A corporation is a separate legal entity with its own obligations. Your accountant and your college can tell you what fits your situation.
