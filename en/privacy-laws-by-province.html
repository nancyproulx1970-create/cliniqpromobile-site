Health privacy laws by province: what applies to your private practice

In private practice, the law that protects your patients' information depends on your province. Here is a simple map, from British Columbia to the Atlantic, and what CliniQ Pro does to help you meet it.

British Columbia

Personal Information Protection Act (PIPA)

For professionals in private practice. Your college (for nurses, the BCCNM) adds its own rules on records.

Alberta

Health Information Act (HIA), or PIPA

The HIA applies to the custodians it names; if you are not one of them, it is generally Alberta’s PIPA.

Saskatchewan

Health Information Protection Act (HIPA)

Applies to “trustees”, which include health professionals.

Manitoba

Personal Health Information Act (PHIA)

Applies to “trustees”, which include health professionals.

Ontario

Personal Health Information Protection Act (PHIPA)

A regulated health professional who keeps patient information in her practice is a “health information custodian”.

Quebec

Law 25 and the Act respecting health and social services information (Law 5)

Consent, privacy officer, incident register, privacy impact assessment.

New Brunswick

Personal Health Information Privacy and Access Act (PHIPAA)

Applies to custodians, including health care providers.

Nova Scotia

Personal Health Information Act (PHIA)

Applies to custodians, including regulated health professionals.

Prince Edward Island

Health Information Act

Applies to custodians, including health care providers.

Newfoundland and Labrador

Personal Health Information Act (PHIA)

Applies to custodians, including health care professionals.

Elsewhere in Canada

PIPEDA (federal)

When no provincial law applies to a commercial activity.

What these laws ask of you, almost everywhere

  • Ask for consent and say why you collect each piece of information.
  • Protect the records: limited access, locked devices, safe storage.
  • Keep the records as long as your college requires, then destroy them safely.
  • Let patients see and correct their information.
  • Know what to do if information is lost or stolen.

How CliniQ Pro helps

  • Data hosted in Canada (Montréal region), and a backup copy in Canada (Toronto).
  • Each professional sees only her own records; the server refuses any other read.
  • Signed notes that can’t be changed, and consents recorded with their date.
  • An access log that can be neither changed nor erased.
  • You can export your records and close your account yourself, from the app.

How long to keep records

The retention period is set by your professional college and your province, not by the app. Check with your college: CliniQ Pro keeps your records until you decide, and lets you export them.

General information, not legal advice. Laws change: check with your college or a lawyer for your own situation.

Frequently asked questions

Which privacy law applies to me in Ontario?

In Ontario, a regulated health professional who keeps patient information in her practice is a health information custodian under PHIPA. CliniQ Pro is built to help you meet it: data in Canada, access limited to your own records, signed notes, dated consents and an access log.

Which privacy law applies to my private practice in British Columbia?

In BC, private practices are generally governed by the Personal Information Protection Act (PIPA). Your college, such as the BCCNM for nurses, adds its own record-keeping rules.

Does CliniQ Pro keep my data in Canada?

Yes. Your data is hosted in Canada, in the Montréal region, and the backup copy of photos and documents is kept in Canada, in Toronto.
