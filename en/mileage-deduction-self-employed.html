Deducting your mileage when you are self-employed in health care (2026)

Are you a nurse, physiotherapist, massage therapist or other self-employed health professional who travels to patients? Your car expenses are deductible, on one condition: you must prove your work kilometres.

Short answer: when you are self-employed, you do not deduct 73¢ per kilometre. You deduct your actual vehicle expenses × (work km ÷ total km for the year), backed by a logbook of your trips. CliniQ Pro keeps that logbook for you and calculates your vehicle's business-use share. The CliniQ Pro software and app keep that logbook for you, anywhere in Canada.

The 73¢/km rate is not for you

In 2026, the CRA lets an employer pay up to 73¢/km for the first 5,000 kilometres, then 67¢/km, tax-free (77¢ and 71¢ in the territories). That rule is for employees. When you are self-employed, you deduct your actual expenses, based on the share used for work.

The calculation

(work km ÷ total km for the year) × vehicle expenses

Example: 12,000 work km out of 20,000 km in total is 60%. With $8,000 in vehicle expenses, you deduct $4,800. Parking paid for work is added in full.

Expenses that count

Fuel or electric charging, maintenance and repairs, insurance, registration and licence, car washes, interest on a car loan or lease payments, and depreciation (capital cost allowance). Some 2026 limits apply:

2026 limitAmount
Cost of a passenger vehicle for CCA (Class 10.1)$39,000 before tax
Zero-emission vehicle (Class 54)$61,000 before tax
Lease payments$1,100 per month before tax
Interest on a car loan$350 per month

Federally, these expenses go on line 9281 of form T2125 (Chart A), and depreciation on line 9936. In Quebec, they go on form TP-80.

What your logbook must include

For each work trip: the date, the destination, the purpose and the number of kilometres. Also record the odometer at the start and end of the year to know your total kilometres. Without a logbook, the whole deduction can be refused in an audit. Keep your receipts and logbook for six years.

Which trips count

Travel between home and your clinic or regular place of work is personal and does not count. Trips to patients' homes, between visits, or to a supplier for work do count. If your main office is in your home, the trip from home to a patient is a work trip.

Frequently asked questions

Can I deduct 73¢ per kilometre if I am self-employed?

No. The 2026 rate of 73¢/km (67¢/km after 5,000 km) is the limit an employer can pay an employee tax-free. When you are self-employed, you deduct your actual vehicle expenses based on the share of kilometres driven for work.

How do I calculate the business-use share of my vehicle?

Divide your work kilometres for the year by your total kilometres for the year, from the odometer. Then multiply that percentage by your vehicle expenses. Parking paid for work is fully deductible.

What must my mileage logbook include?

For each work trip: the date, the destination, the purpose and the number of kilometres. Also record the odometer at the start and end of the year. Without a logbook, the deduction can be refused in an audit.

Does the trip between home and my clinic count?

No. Travel between home and your regular place of work is personal. Trips to your patients' homes are work trips.

Is it the same everywhere in Canada?

Yes, the CRA method is the same in every province and territory. In Quebec, Revenu Québec applies the same method on the provincial return.
